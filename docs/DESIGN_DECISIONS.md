# Design decisions

[Research](RESEARCH.md) · [Case study](CASE_STUDY.md)

| Evidence or design concern | Design response | Evidence in the delivered work | Remaining question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The review can leave users unsure how to leave or return | Persistent navigation, Exit, Back, Continue, progress dots | Wireframes, high-fidelity screens, and prototype | Can users recover independently from any review step? |
| Terms and acronyms need explanation | Glossary-style explanation links and FAQ | Portfolio rationale and visible prototype screens | Are definitions complete, discoverable, and understandable? |
| A result needs context | A Market area and explanatory copy alongside status information | High-fidelity Market and funding screens; Market link opened | Can users distinguish general context from account-specific information? |
| Primary actions need to be recognizable | Larger and more prominent buttons and clear labels | Wireframe commentary and UI guide | Are targets, focus, and contrast sufficient across devices? |
| Users need a route from summary to action | Checklist and next-step content | Original checklist screen and planned flow | Do people understand which controls merely explain and which would change an account? |
| A financial interface can feel impersonal | Warm illustrations, conversational copy, and pale content surfaces | High-fidelity designs | Does the tone support understanding without implying guaranteed safety or outcomes? |
| Information inconsistencies can reduce confidence | A future content and scenario-data review | Research theme and proposed validation plan | Are amounts, dates, labels, and missing-data explanations coherent across all screens? |

The last row is a follow-up requirement, not a claimed completed fix. The prototype contains illustrative data and financial copy that still need validation. No improvement percentage, participant success rate, or accessibility certification is inferred from these design choices.
