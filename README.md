Vanguard Year in Review explores how an annual financial summary can become easier to read, understand, and navigate. My redesign focuses on adults aged 50+, combining a clear reading sequence with explanations, visible navigation, and a more supportive visual tone.

## The problem

A financial report can look clear while still leaving important questions unanswered: What does this term mean? Why did the result change? What should I do next? How do I return to the dashboard or ask for help?

The project treats understanding and a sense of control as design priorities. It preserves a familiar financial interface while making explanations, navigation, and next actions easier to find.

## Design direction

- **A visible route through the review.** Persistent navigation, an exit, progress indicators, and Back/Continue controls make movement explicit.
- **Context alongside the numbers.** Explanatory copy, glossary-style links, and a Market section give the information a clearer frame.
- **A readable visual hierarchy.** Larger and more prominent controls, vertical reading sequences, and warm illustrations organize the experience.
- **A clear way forward.** Action checklists, help cues, and FAQ access connect the review to follow-up questions and tasks.

These are design responses, not measured claims of increased trust, reduced anxiety, or improved financial outcomes. Research concerned the earlier experience; a comparative evaluation of the final redesign is not documented.

## Outcome

The completed design work connects research synthesis to a structure diagram, wireframes, a UI style guide, high-fidelity screens, and an accessible-to-view Figma prototype. The review entry, Market navigation, and FAQ navigation were directly opened during preparation of this case study.

This repository contains design documentation and visual deliverables. Open the Markdown files directly or follow the prototype link; no software installation is needed. The original screens retain their source proportions and do not include external device black borders.

Financial names, amounts, charts, and probability figures shown in the prototype are illustrative design content, not measured usability results or validated financial forecasts. Participant identities, recordings, and raw research records are excluded.
